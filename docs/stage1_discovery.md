### Where the accounts disagree

| The disagreement | Who says what | How the data settles it | What follows |
|---|---|---|---|
| How much of tier two's queue was avoidable | Daniel says about half. Marcus does not mention it and reports escalation as a volume problem | 49.1% of escalated tickets (138 of 281) were answerable from the documentation | Daniel is right, and the escalation rate is largely a findability and confidence problem rather than a difficulty problem. This is the core of the reframe |
| Whether non-fluent English tickets are served worse | Sofia says they have the worst satisfaction scores and nobody has noticed | Non-fluent CSAT 3.04 against fluent 2.95; FCR 45.8% against 43.2%; median 198 min against 216 | Contradicted. Sofia is describing her own workload accurately and the customer outcome inaccurately. Treated as a workload finding, not a fairness one |
| Which tier gets the best service | Ravi believes enterprise answers arrive in about an hour and that he, on business, waits longer. Marcus fears enterprise service degrading | Enterprise median 369 min, business 141, standard 266. Enterprise FCR lowest at 37.3%; only 37% of enterprise tickets resolve within an hour | Contradicted and inverted. Enterprise is the worst-served tier. Marcus's principal worry points the wrong way, and protecting the status quo for enterprise would protect the worst outcome in the business |
| Whether the knowledge base is stale | Daniel warns that circulating answers were correct two years ago. Ines says her 29 articles are accurate within the review cycle | Every article was reviewed within the current cycle | Both are right about different things. The staleness is in the agents' private files, which is direct evidence for grounding in the reviewed corpus and excluding past agent replies |

## 6. The problem statement

In `docs/problem_statement.md`. Summary:

> CloudServe is not short of answers. It is short of a reliable path from what a customer wrote to the article that already answers them. Seven in ten tickets are answerable from twenty-nine accurate, currently reviewed articles, yet agents reconstruct those answers from memory because search matches titles and customers do not use the words the titles use. The cost is paid twice: as an 8–12 hour first response while known answers are rewritten by hand, and as a 56% escalation rate of which roughly half is not difficulty but an agent who could not find the page and would not send an answer they were not confident about. The organisation cannot see this because each person sees only their own part of it.

Every claim in it points at a row above or a section of the findings. The sentence none of the five could have produced on their own is the last one: each of them holds a piece, and nobody had assembled it.
